Good circulation is vital for healing and performance. Blood flow restriction training is one way therapists help patients build strength when heavy lifting isn't an option.

## What blood flow restriction training is

Blood Flow Restriction (BFR) training uses a cuff, similar to a blood pressure cuff, to partly restrict blood flow out of a working muscle while blood still flows in. Combined with light exercise, this triggers responses in the body that improve muscle strength, endurance and size.

Light exercise on its own usually doesn't build much strength. With BFR, it can produce results closer to high-intensity training. That makes it useful for people with joint problems or injuries who can't safely lift heavy yet.

### Benefits

- **New capillary growth:** BFR stimulates growth factors that can help form new small blood vessels.
- **Better oxygen delivery:** the cycle of restriction and release can improve how your blood carries oxygen.
- **Blood vessel health:** regular BFR training may improve blood vessel function.
- **Muscle pump:** the training drives blood flow to the targeted muscles.
- **Faster clearing of waste:** metabolites that build up during restriction are flushed out when the cuff is released.

### Risks and considerations

BFR isn't risk-free. The pressure has to be set precisely: too loose does nothing, and too tight can cause numbness, tissue damage or even blood clots. Always talk with your doctor first, and only do BFR training under the guidance of a qualified professional.
