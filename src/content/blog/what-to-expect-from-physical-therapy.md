Starting physical therapy is a big step toward feeling better. Whether you're dealing with a new injury, chronic pain or recovery after surgery, knowing what happens at PT takes the guesswork out of it. Physical therapy isn't just about recovering. It's about giving you the knowledge and skills to stay healthy long term.

## What physical therapy is

Physical therapy diagnoses and treats problems with how your body moves. Therapists use exercise, hands-on manual therapy, education and other techniques to improve movement, strength and function. Every patient gets an assessment and a plan built for their situation, using evidence-based treatment whether the problem came from an injury, an illness or aging.

## Why you might need it

Physical therapy is commonly recommended for recovering from injuries or surgery, managing chronic conditions, improving balance and preventing falls, improving mobility and flexibility, and managing pain without surgery or medication.

## Your first visit

Your first session starts with an evaluation. Your therapist will:

- Review your medical history
- Talk through your current symptoms
- Test your strength, flexibility and range of motion

Then you'll set goals together, both short-term and long-term: less pain, more strength, better mobility, getting back to a specific activity. Clear goals make it easy to track progress and stay motivated.

Wear comfortable clothes you can move in, and bring your questions.

## Your treatment plan

Based on your evaluation and goals, your therapist builds a plan that may include:

- **Exercise:** targeted exercises for strength, flexibility and endurance, adjusted as you improve.
- **Manual therapy:** hands-on techniques like soft tissue work and joint mobilization to relieve pain and improve movement.
- **Education:** body mechanics and posture, so you know how to move safely and prevent re-injury.
- **Modalities:** heat, cold, ultrasound or electrical stimulation to reduce pain and inflammation.

## Ongoing sessions

Most patients come in a few times a week, depending on their condition. Each session builds on the last, and your therapist adjusts the plan as you progress. You'll also get a home exercise program so you keep improving between visits. Showing up consistently and doing your home program makes the biggest difference in your results.

## The road to recovery

Physical therapy is a partnership. Your therapist tracks your progress and adjusts the plan, and you put in the work. Celebrate the milestones along the way, whether that's less pain, better movement or getting back to something you love.
